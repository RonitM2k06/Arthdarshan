A mutual fund pools money from many people. You own units. NAV (net asset value) is the value of one unit: what the fund owns minus what it owes, divided by the number of units. A low NAV does not mean cheap and a high NAV does not mean expensive; it is a measurement, not a forecast.

Exit load is a charge for taking money out within a stated period. Expense ratio is a yearly charge. A yield figure described as indicative or estimated is not a promise. Credit risk means a borrower held by the fund may fail to repay, so a debt fund is not risk-free. A riskometer label tells you the product's stated risk level.

Before signing ask: what can I lose, when and how can I withdraw and at what cost, and what are all the charges in rupees? If you cannot get clear written answers, do not sign yet. Technical words do not make a product credible; you can ask for plain language.
