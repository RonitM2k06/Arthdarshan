Concentration risk is the share of your money exposed to one company, sector or idea. If one fictional company is all of your simulated money and it falls 35%, you lose 35% of everything. If it is a quarter of your money, the same fall costs about 9% of the total.

Diversification means spreading money across different things so that one failure does less damage. It reduces concentration risk but does not remove risk: in a broad downturn many assets can fall together. Two very similar assets, such as two companies in the same sector, are not truly diversified.

A useful question for any plan: if my biggest piece fell by 40%, how much of my total would I lose? If the answer is more than you could handle, the plan is concentrated. An easily accessible cushion for emergencies is also part of a resilient plan.
