Saving means keeping money aside for emergencies and goals, with safety and easy access as the priority. Investing accepts some risk in the hope of growth. Many planners suggest first building an emergency cushion that covers several months of expenses.

Inflation is the general rise in prices over time. At an illustrative 6% a year, something costing Rs 100 today would cost about Rs 106 next year, and a Rs 1,000 basket would cost about Rs 1,791 after 10 years. Money that grows more slowly than prices loses buying power even if the rupee number rises.

Compounding means earning growth on earlier growth. Rs 10,000 at a hypothetical 8% a year becomes about Rs 14,693 after 5 years and Rs 21,589 after 10 years. Time does most of the work, which is why costs and debts that compound work against you too. These numbers are illustrative, not forecasts.
