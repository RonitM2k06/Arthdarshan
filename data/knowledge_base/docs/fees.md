Fees are what you pay to hold or use a financial product. They come off what you keep, whether or not the product does well.

Common kinds: an entry charge taken at the start; a yearly charge (often shown as an expense ratio) taken from the value every year; an exit charge if you leave early; brokerage and other transaction costs. Marketing often leads with the best-case growth figure and puts charges in small print.

A yearly charge compounds against you because it is taken from a balance that is supposed to be growing. Illustration with hypothetical numbers: Rs 1,00,000 growing at 8% a year for 20 years reaches about Rs 4,66,096. With a 2.5% yearly charge the same period gives about Rs 2,91,776. Ask for every charge in rupees and percentages, in writing, and compare options on the same assumptions. Lower charges do not guarantee better returns; they remove one certain drag.
